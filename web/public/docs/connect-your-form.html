Connect your website form to a flow

A webhook-triggered flow waits for your form to send it each new submission. Your form needs two things: the flow's webhook address and an authorization header that proves the request is allowed. You'll find both in your flow under Settings → Triggers.

The easiest route: Zapier or Make (works with any form)

Most form tools (Google Forms, Squarespace, Wix) can't attach a custom header on their own. A no-code bridge solves this in a few clicks and works with virtually every form:

  1. In Zapier or Make, create a new automation (a "Zap" / "Scenario").
  2. Pick your form as the trigger step — e.g. "New response in Google Forms", "New entry in Typeform".
  3. Add an action step: in Zapier choose Webhooks by Zapier → POST; in Make choose HTTP → Make a request.
  4. Set the URL to your flow's webhook address, and the method to POST.
  5. Add a header named Authorization with the value Bearer <your secret> (copy it from the Triggers panel).
  6. Map your form fields into the request body.
  7. Turn the automation on, then submit your form once to test it.

Typeform (direct)

Typeform can call a webhook itself: open your form → Connect → Webhooks → Add a webhook, and paste the webhook address. Adding the Authorization header requires a paid Typeform plan; on a free plan, use the Zapier/Make route above.

Google Forms

Easiest via Zapier/Make using the "New response" trigger. If you're comfortable with a little scripting: in your form's spreadsheet, open Extensions → Apps Script and use UrlFetchApp.fetch to POST to the webhook address with the header.

Squarespace / Wix

These don't send webhooks on their own. Connect them through Zapier or Make using their "new form submission" trigger.

Test it before you rely on it

In the flow editor, use Send test event to fire the flow with a sample submission and watch each step light up — no need to submit your real form while you're still setting things up.

Going live: the webhook address points at wherever the daemon is reachable. While testing locally that's localhost; replace it with your public URL before you point a real form at it.
